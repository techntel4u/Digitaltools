Google Classroom10–15 min

Practice Sets: which question did this room find hardest?

Learners get help at the moment they get stuck, not only when you reach their table.

Create one

  1. Open your class, go to Classwork → Create → Assignment.
  2. Under Attach, choose Practice sets → Create.
  3. Click Edit, pick a question type (short answer, paragraph, single or multi-select), and enter the question and correct answer. Click New question for the next one.
  4. Add support: open Resources for hints or a YouTube video on the whole set, or Extra help beside a single question.
  5. Click Try as student to check the hints and flow.
  6. Click Done editing, then attach the set to your assignment.

Already have a Google Form quiz? Use Insert content → Import to bring the questions in. Once a set is assigned it can't be edited, so check it first.

What you'll see

  • Instant feedback on every answer
  • Hints when learners are stuck
  • Which questions the group found hardest
  • A snapshot per learner and per question

Who it helps: learners who won't put a hand up. Help arrives in the moment, privately.

Google Classroom15–20 min

Interactive Video: questions pop up inside the video

You check understanding while learners watch, not just at the end.

Create one

  1. Go to Classwork → Create → Assignment and add a title.
  2. Under Attach, click YouTube, then search or paste a video link.
  3. Click Add questions, then + Add. Choose multiple choice, checkboxes or open-ended.
  4. Set when each question appears using the timestamp or Question time. Click Save and continue.
  5. Only need part of the video? Set a start and end time under Details.
  6. Click Try as a student, then attach it to the assignment.

Look for the spark icon under a video title: Gemini can suggest questions for some English-language videos. Check and edit every one.

Why it works

  • Checks understanding while learners watch
  • Learners can pause and rewatch at their own pace
  • You see who engaged and who skipped ahead
  • Great for showing real-world applications

Who it helps: ESOL learners, learners with slower processing, and anyone who missed the lesson.

Google ClassroomDifferentiation

Differentiate with student groups

Set up groups once, then send different versions of a task, such as a set with extra hints or a shorter video clip, to the learners who need them. Nobody else sees who got what.

  1. Open the People page of your class and create a student group.
  2. When you create an assignment, choose that group instead of all students.

Wayground20–28 min

From your slides to an interactive lesson

An existing deck, uploaded, with AI-suggested questions between the slides. You answer all the way through, not just at the end.

How it works

  1. Upload an existing presentation.
  2. AI suggests questions to go between your slides.
  3. Check and edit every question.
  4. Launch it live with the group.
  5. Read the end report.

The AI is a starting point, not the final word. Check every question, especially technical vocabulary in vocational subjects.

As we go, notice

  • Answering on your own device
  • The room's answers appearing live
  • What the AI got right, and what it didn't
  • How it feels as a learner

WaygroundAbout 5 minutes

Upload a slide deck and let AI suggest the questions

  1. Log in at wayground.com with your college email.
  2. Click Create and choose Presentation.
  3. Upload the demo deck, or one of your own (PowerPoint, Google Slides or PDF).
  4. In the Select your imported slides window, leave Auto-generate questions based on your teaching goals switched on.
  5. Pick a goal: Teach checks for understanding, Review recaps key learning, Practice reinforces topics covered. Then click Import.
  6. Wayground adds suggested questions between your slides. Open each one and check it: keep it, edit it or delete it.
  7. Preview it as a learner, then click Start now to run it live or Assign it to a class.

The AI is a starting point, not the final word. Check every question, especially technical vocabulary in vocational subjects.

Work in Google Slides? The Wayground for Google Slides add-on adds questions inside Slides itself.

Extra: add each question type yourself

The demo deck uses a different question type after every slide. To add one, open your presentation in the editor, use the add option after the slide, and choose the question type. Each card below gives the exact question used in the demo and what to enter.

Poll

"How many hours a day do you spend online?"

  1. Add a question after the slide and choose Poll.
  2. Type the question and the options: Less than 2 · 2–4 · 4–6 · More than 6.
  3. There's no correct answer to mark, so everyone can take part.

Why: a no-wrong-answer warm-up that gives you a quick read of the room.

Multiple choice

"You delete a post an hour after sharing it. Is it gone from your digital footprint?"

  1. Add a question and choose Multiple choice.
  2. Add four options: Yes, deleted means gone · No, it may already be screenshotted, shared or saved · Only if nobody liked it · Yes, after 24 hours.
  3. Mark the second option as correct.

Why: an instant check that tackles a common misconception.

Categorise

"Sort these into ACTIVE or PASSIVE footprint."

  1. Add a question and choose Categorise.
  2. Create two categories: Active and Passive.
  3. Add items to each. Active: Posting a TikTok, Leaving a Google review, Signing up for a giveaway. Passive: An app tracking your location, Cookies on a shopping site, Your IP address being logged.

Why: learners apply the idea, and the results show which items confuse them.

Open-ended

"An employer searches your name tomorrow. What would you want them to find, and what would you NOT want them to find?"

  1. Add a question and choose Open-ended.
  2. Type the question. There's no answer to mark.
  3. When you run it live, show the answers on screen anonymously to start a discussion.

Why: every learner gets a voice, including the quieter ones.

Hotspot

"Click on the part of the post that tells a burglar the house will be empty."

  1. Download the post image (a fictional social media post).
  2. Add a question, choose Hotspot, and upload the image.
  3. Mark the correct area: the caption "house empty for 2 weeks".
  4. Optional: run it again for the location tag, door number, full name on the college ID, and the "Public" setting.

Why: visual and hands-on, so it suits learners with lower reading confidence.

Fill in the blank

"The National Cyber Security Centre recommends a password made of ___ random words."

  1. Add a question and choose Fill in the blank.
  2. Type the sentence with the blank.
  3. Set the answer as three, and add 3 as an accepted answer too.

Why: a quick recall check with no options to guess from.

Match

"Match each letter of THINK to its word."

  1. Add a question and choose Match.
  2. Add five pairs: T = True · H = Helpful · I = Inspiring · N = Necessary · K = Kind.

Why: a low-stakes memory check that works as a quick-fire round.

Reorder

"Put these steps in the right order."

  1. Add a question and choose Reorder.
  2. Enter the steps in the correct order: Delete the post · Ask friends to remove shares or tags · Tighten privacy settings · Search your name · Talk to someone you trust.
  3. Wayground shuffles them for learners.

Why: checks understanding of a process, not just facts.

Draw

"Draw or write one thing you will change about your online profile this week."

  1. Add a question and choose Draw.
  2. Type the prompt. Learners can draw or write their answer.
  3. Use it as the exit ticket at the end of the lesson.

Why: a creative exit ticket that gives learners who struggle with writing another way to respond.

Want to explore further? Try adding a Video, a Passage or a Website link to your presentation, for example a short news clip about a data breach, or a page from the NCSC site.

Discuss · 3 min

What does the report tell us?

Every activity you run creates a report, so you can see what the group understood without marking anything.

Open the report

  1. In Wayground, click Sessions in the left-hand menu.
  2. Click the activity you've just run.

What to look at

  • Question by question: which questions most of the group got wrong.
  • Each learner: their score, accuracy and how long they took on each question.

Talk about it with the person next to you

  1. Which questions did the group get wrong?
  2. Which learner would you check in with?
  3. What would you reteach on Monday?

Your answers are your next lesson plan.

Accommodations: support without singling anyone out

What are accommodations?

Accommodations are supports you switch on for individual learners, such as read aloud, extra time or translation. The whole group does the same activity, but each learner gets it in a way they can access. Nobody else in the room can see who has what.

Where to find them

  1. Open an activity and click Start now or Assign.
  2. On the settings page, find Accommodations.
  3. Choose the learner and switch on the supports they need.

Set them once and they apply automatically every time that learner takes part.
