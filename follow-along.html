Google for Education

Everything here is already in Google Classroom. No new login and no extra cost. If you can set an assignment, you can do this.

Google Classroom10–15 min

Practice Sets: which question did this room find hardest?

Learners get help at the moment they get stuck, not only when you reach their table.

Create one

  1. Open your class, go to Classwork → Create → Assignment.
  2. Under Attach, choose Practice sets → Create.
  3. Click Edit, pick a question type (short answer, paragraph, single or multi-select), and enter the question and correct answer. Click New question for the next one.
  4. Add support: open Resources for hints or a YouTube video on the whole set, or Extra help beside a single question.
  5. Click Try as student to check the hints and flow.
  6. Click Done editing, then attach the set to your assignment.

Already have a Google Form quiz? Use Insert content → Import to bring the questions in. Once a set is assigned it can't be edited, so check it first.

What you'll see

  • Instant feedback on every answer
  • Hints when learners are stuck
  • Which questions the group found hardest
  • A snapshot per learner and per question

Who it helps: learners who won't put a hand up. Help arrives in the moment, privately.

Google Classroom15–20 min

Interactive Video: questions pop up inside the video

You check understanding while learners watch, not just at the end.

Create one

  1. Go to Classwork → Create → Assignment and add a title.
  2. Under Attach, click YouTube, then search or paste a video link.
  3. Click Add questions, then + Add. Choose multiple choice, checkboxes or open-ended.
  4. Set when each question appears using the timestamp or Question time. Click Save and continue.
  5. Only need part of the video? Set a start and end time under Details.
  6. Click Try as a student, then attach it to the assignment.

Look for the spark icon under a video title: Gemini can suggest questions for some English-language videos. Check and edit every one.

Why it works

  • Checks understanding while learners watch
  • Learners can pause and rewatch at their own pace
  • You see who engaged and who skipped ahead
  • Great for showing real-world applications

Who it helps: ESOL learners, learners with slower processing, and anyone who missed the lesson.

Google ClassroomDifferentiation

Differentiate with student groups

Set up groups once, then send different versions of a task, such as a set with extra hints or a shorter video clip, to the learners who need them. Nobody else sees who got what.

  1. Open the People page of your class and create a student group.
  2. When you create an assignment, choose that group instead of all students.

Wayground20–28 min

From your slides to an interactive lesson

An existing deck, uploaded, with AI-suggested questions between the slides. You answer all the way through, not just at the end.

How it works

  1. Upload an existing presentation.
  2. AI suggests questions to go between your slides.
  3. Check and edit every question.
  4. Launch it live with the group.
  5. Read the end report.

The AI is a starting point, not the final word. Check every question, especially technical vocabulary in vocational subjects.

As we go, notice

  • Answering on your own device
  • The room's answers appearing live
  • What the AI got right, and what it didn't
  • How it feels as a learner

The demo lesson: one question type per slide

The Your Digital Footprint deck uses a different Wayground question type after every slide. Use it as a menu when you plan your own: each one says why that type fits that moment.

Download the sample deck (.pptx, 9 MB)

Poll

"How many hours a day do you spend online?"

A no-wrong-answer warm-up. Everyone takes part and you get a quick read of the room.

Multiple choice

"You delete a post an hour after sharing it. Is it gone?"

An instant check for understanding that tackles a common misconception.

Categorise

"Sort these into active or passive footprint."

Learners apply the idea instead of just recalling it. Results show which items cause confusion.

Open-ended

"An employer searches your name tomorrow. What would you want them to find?"

Gives every learner a voice, including quieter ones. Show answers anonymously to start a discussion.

Hotspot

"Click the part of the post that tells a burglar the house will be empty."

Visual and hands-on, which suits learners with lower reading confidence.

Fill in the blank

"The NCSC recommends a password made of ___ random words."

A quick recall check with no options to guess from.

Match

"Match each letter of THINK to its word."

A low-stakes memory check that works well as a quick-fire round.

Reorder

"Put these steps in the right order."

Checks understanding of a process, not just facts.

Multiple choice or poll

"Who in college should you tell if something online is worrying you?"

Makes sure every learner leaves knowing where to go for help.

Draw (exit ticket)

"Draw or write one thing you'll change about your online profile this week."

A creative exit ticket. Drawing gives learners who struggle with writing another way in.

Accommodations: support without singling anyone out

Set supports for each learner once and they apply automatically every time that learner takes part. Nobody else in the room can see them. The learner who needs support just gets the same lesson in a way they can access.

Look for Accommodations on the settings page when you start or assign an activity.

Basic accommodations

Learning environment and maths

Ask yourself: which learner in your group would this help? Read aloud suits learners working towards Functional Skills English; translation suits ESOL and EAL learners; extra time and fewer options mirror access arrangements many learners already have.
